📖 Understanding the Basics

What Is Climate Change?

Climate change refers to long-term shifts in global temperatures and weather patterns. While some changes occur naturally, since the 1800s, human activities have been the main driver of climate change — primarily through the burning of fossil fuels like coal, oil, and gas.

Unlike weather, which describes short-term atmospheric conditions (like today's rain or sunshine), climate describes the average conditions over a long period — typically 30 years or more. When we talk about climate change, we're referring to these long-term shifts.

Global warming is the long-term heating of Earth's climate system observed since the pre-industrial period (between 1850 and 1900). It is primarily driven by human activities, especially fossil fuel burning, which increases heat-trapping greenhouse gas levels in Earth's atmosphere.

What Is Climate?

Climate is the average pattern of weather over a long period. Climate change means these patterns are shifting significantly due to human actions.

⛅

Weather vs. Climate

Weather is what you see outside on any given day. Climate is the average of weather over 30+ years. "Climate is what you expect; weather is what you get."

🌡️

Global Warming

Earth's average temperature has risen by ~1.1°C since pre-industrial times. Even small increases cause massive disruptions to ecosystems and human societies.

🏭

Greenhouse Effect

Greenhouse gases (CO₂, CH₄, N₂O) trap heat from the Sun in our atmosphere. Human activities are intensifying this natural effect, causing the planet to overheat.

    Why Does This Matter?

    The greenhouse effect is natural and essential. Without it, Earth's surface would average a freezing −18°C instead of the life-supporting +15°C we enjoy today.

    The problem: human activities are adding extra greenhouse gases far beyond natural levels. This enhanced greenhouse effect is driving global temperatures upward at a rate unprecedented in human history.

    Even 1.5°C of warming above pre-industrial levels — a threshold we are dangerously close to crossing — is projected to trigger extreme weather, coral reef collapse, and food insecurity for hundreds of millions worldwide.

    What Do These Trends Mean?

    Long-term climate trends are far more meaningful than individual weather events or single-year measurements. Climate scientists study patterns across decades — not single days or seasons.

    A cold winter in one region does not contradict global warming; it is the consistent, decade-long rise in global average temperatures that defines climate change. Similarly, year-to-year fluctuations in CO₂ or renewables matter less than the clear directional trend visible across the charts above.

    Understanding these long-term trends — and the human activities driving them — is the essential first step toward informed climate action.

    🚧 Separate Truth from Fiction

    Myth vs Fact

    Climate change is surrounded by misinformation. Reveal the science-based truth behind some of the most common misconceptions.

    🚨 MYTH

    "Climate change is a natural cycle. Humans have nothing to do with it."

    ✅ Fact: While Earth has experienced natural climate cycles over millions of years, the current rate of warming is far faster than any natural cycle. Scientific evidence shows that human activities — especially burning fossil fuels — are the dominant cause of warming since the mid-20th century.
    🚨 MYTH

    "CO₂ is just a trace gas — it can't possibly affect the entire climate."

    ✅ Fact: Even in small concentrations, CO₂ is highly effective at trapping heat. Pre-industrial CO₂ was around 280 ppm; it is now over 420 ppm — a 50% increase that has measurably raised global average temperatures.
    🚨 MYTH

    "Scientists disagree about whether climate change is real."

    ✅ Fact: There is overwhelming scientific consensus — over 97% of actively publishing climate scientists agree that human-caused climate change is real and a serious threat. Major scientific organisations worldwide share this position.
    🚨 MYTH

    "Individual actions don't matter — only governments can fix climate change."

    ✅ Fact: Individual choices — diet, transport, energy use, and consumption — collectively account for a significant share of emissions. While systemic policy is essential, widespread individual action accelerates change and signals demand for greener products and services.
    🚨 MYTH

    "Renewable energy is too unreliable to replace fossil fuels."

    ✅ Fact: Solar and wind energy, combined with modern battery storage and smart grids, can provide reliable, continuous power. Many countries and regions already run on high percentages of renewable energy. Costs have dropped dramatically over the past decade.
    🚨 MYTH

    "Climate change only affects polar bears and icebergs — not everyday people."

    ✅ Fact: Climate change affects food and water security, public health, extreme weather events, displacement, and economies worldwide. Vulnerable communities — often those least responsible for emissions — are already experiencing its severe impacts.
    💡 Take Action

    Climate Tips

    Simple, practical actions that can make a real difference — one choice at a time.

    ⚡

    Energy

    Small changes in how you use electricity at home can reduce your carbon footprint meaningfully over time.

    • Turn off lights, screens, and appliances when not in use.
    • Switch to LED bulbs — they use up to 80% less energy than incandescent bulbs.
    • Set your thermostat a degree or two lower in winter and higher in summer.
    • Choose energy-efficient appliances (look for energy-rating labels).
    • Unplug chargers and standby devices — they draw power even when idle.
    🚌

    Transport

    How you get around is one of the biggest factors in your personal carbon footprint.

    • Walk or cycle for short journeys where it is safe and practical.
    • Use public transport instead of driving when available.
    • Carpool with colleagues or neighbours when driving is necessary.
    • Combine errands into one trip to reduce overall distance driven.
    • Consider hybrid or electric vehicles when replacing a car.
    🌱

    Food

    Food production accounts for a significant share of global emissions. Thoughtful choices help.

    • Reduce food waste — plan meals and store food properly.
    • Try including more plant-based meals in your week when practical.
    • Buy local and seasonal produce where available.
    • Compost food scraps instead of sending them to landfill.
    • Avoid single-use packaging when shopping.
    ♻️

    Waste

    Reducing what we throw away lowers the energy and resources needed to process waste.

    • Reduce unnecessary purchases — ask yourself if you really need an item.
    • Reuse bags, containers, and packaging rather than buying new ones.
    • Recycle correctly according to your local council’s guidelines.
    • Repair items before replacing them when possible.
    • Donate or sell usable items rather than discarding them.
    💧

    Water

    Treating and pumping water uses energy. Using water carefully reduces both water and energy waste.

    • Turn off the tap while brushing teeth or washing dishes.
    • Take shorter showers when practical.
    • Fix dripping taps and report leaks promptly.
    • Use a washing machine with a full load rather than partial loads.
    • Collect rainwater for watering plants if possible.
    🌍

    Everyday Choices

    Everyday decisions about what we buy and how we live add up to a significant collective impact.

    • Buy durable, quality products rather than cheap disposable ones.
    • Reuse what you already own before buying something new.
    • Support businesses and brands with genuine environmental commitments.
    • Talk to friends and family about climate-friendly choices — social norms matter.
    • Stay informed and encourage responsible environmental policy.
    ⚠️ Root Causes

    What Causes Climate Change?

    Human activities are the primary driver of climate change. Here are the major sources contributing to rising greenhouse gas emissions worldwide.

    🏭

    Burning Fossil Fuels

    Coal, oil, and natural gas combustion for energy and electricity is the largest source of greenhouse gas emissions — responsible for about 75% of global emissions.

    #1 Cause · 75% of Emissions
    🌳

    Deforestation

    Forests absorb CO₂. Cutting and burning them releases stored carbon back into the atmosphere while removing our planet's natural carbon sinks.

    Land Use · 10–15% of Emissions
    🚗

    Transportation

    Cars, trucks, ships, and aircraft burn vast amounts of fossil fuels. Transportation accounts for approximately 16% of global greenhouse gas emissions.

    Transport · ~16% of Emissions
    ⚙️

    Industrial Activities

    Manufacturing cement, steel, and chemicals releases enormous amounts of CO₂ and other greenhouse gases. Industry accounts for about 21% of global emissions.

    Industry · ~21% of Emissions
    🌾

    Agriculture

    Livestock farming produces methane, a potent greenhouse gas. Rice paddies, fertilizer use, and land clearing for farming all contribute significantly to emissions.

    Food & Land · ~10% of Emissions
    🗑️

    Waste

    Landfills decomposing organic waste produce methane. Wastewater treatment and incineration also release greenhouse gases into the atmosphere.

    Waste Sector · ~3% of Emissions
    🌊 Consequences

    Effects of Climate Change

    Climate change is already affecting every corner of the planet. These are the most visible and alarming consequences we face today.

    🌡️

    Rising Temperatures

    Global average temperatures have risen 1.1°C since the 1800s, with record-breaking heat waves becoming more frequent and intense.

    🧊

    Melting Glaciers

    Glaciers and ice sheets are shrinking at alarming rates. The Arctic is warming nearly four times faster than the rest of the world.

    🌊

    Sea Level Rise

    Global sea levels have risen ~20 cm since 1900 and are accelerating. Coastal cities and low-lying islands face catastrophic flooding risks.

    ⛈️

    Extreme Weather

    Hurricanes, typhoons, and storms are becoming more powerful and frequent as warmer oceans fuel more intense storm systems worldwide.

    🏜️

    Droughts

    Prolonged droughts threaten food security, water supplies, and agriculture in many regions, particularly in Africa and the Mediterranean.

    🌧️

    Floods

    Heavier rainfall events are causing more frequent and devastating floods, destroying infrastructure, displacing millions of people globally.

    🔥

    Wildfires

    Hotter, drier conditions fuel larger and longer wildfire seasons. Millions of acres burn each year, destroying habitats and air quality.

    🦋

    Biodiversity Loss

    Species are going extinct up to 1,000 times the natural rate. Changing habitats, temperature shifts, and acidic oceans threaten countless species.

    💡 The Path Forward

    Solutions to Climate Change

    There is hope. From renewable energy to individual actions, there are many ways we can work together to slow and reverse climate change.

    ☀️

    Renewable Energy

    Transitioning to solar, wind, hydro, and geothermal energy can eliminate fossil fuel dependence. Renewables are now the cheapest source of electricity in history.

    ⚡ Clean Energy
    💡

    Energy Conservation

    Using energy-efficient appliances, improving home insulation, and reducing wasteful consumption can dramatically cut household and industrial emissions.

    🏠 Home Impact
    🚌

    Sustainable Transport

    Electric vehicles, public transit, cycling, and walking reduce transportation emissions. Shifting to EVs powered by renewables can transform the sector.

    🌿 Zero Emissions
    🌳

    Tree Plantation

    Forests are Earth's lungs — planting billions of trees can absorb massive amounts of CO₂. Protecting existing forests is equally critical for carbon storage.

    🌱 Carbon Sink
    ♻️

    Recycling

    Reducing, reusing, and recycling waste decreases landfill methane emissions and reduces the energy needed to produce new materials from scratch.

    ♻️ Circular Economy
    💧

    Water Conservation

    Water treatment and heating requires significant energy. Conserving water, fixing leaks, and using water-efficient appliances reduces overall carbon footprints.
